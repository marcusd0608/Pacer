# Outreach kit: getting your first ten teachers

Goal of outreach is **not** sales. It is ten real conversations, then five teachers who run Pacer in a live class three times.

## Who to contact (in this order)
1. Instructors and TAs you already know at UCI, especially in large intro courses (hand-raising does not work there).
2. Tutors and tutoring-center managers; test-prep and bootcamp instructors. They teach live, control their own budget, and feel the pain of not seeing who is lost.
3. Teachers from your old school and your friends' parents. Warm intros beat cold emails.
4. Department chairs, only after you have two teachers using it.

Make a copy of `docs/targets.csv` and fill in 30 names. Aim to send 20 messages.

## Message: instructor or TA (short)
> Hi [name], I'm a student at [school] building a tool for lectures. In a big class nobody raises a hand, so you can't tell when students are lost. Students tap "still confused" privately and you see which step, never who. Could I get 10 minutes to hear how you handle this today, and show you a 2-minute demo? No sales, I'm trying to learn what actually helps.

## Message: tutor or center manager
> Hi [name], I'm building a live whiteboard for teaching where students can ask the AI privately and you see where the group is stuck, plus it writes the post-session recap for you. I'm looking for 3 tutors to try it free in real sessions and tell me what's missing. Would you be open to a 10-minute call?

## The 10-minute conversation (listen more than you talk)
1. "Walk me through the last class where students were lost. How did you find out?"
2. "What do you do after class? Recaps, emails, practice problems, makeup material?" (Write down the hours per week. This is the pain you can charge for.)
3. "What tools do you use for live teaching today, and what do you wish they did?"
4. Show the 2-minute demo: join, snip, still confused, alert, check-in poll, post-class package.
5. "What would stop you from using this next week?" (Privacy, setup time, students needing accounts, something else.)
6. "If this saved you two hours a week, would you pay $9 a month for it?" Note the exact words they use.
7. "Who else should I talk to?"

## Running a pilot
- Offer to set it up and sit in (or join the call) for their first session. Their first session is the whole battle.
- Ask for three sessions over three weeks. Send a two-line follow-up after each: what you saw in the numbers, one thing you will fix.
- After session three, ask for a one-sentence quote and permission to use it.

## What to record for every teacher
Name, role, class size, sessions run, what they loved, what blocked them, what they said about price. A spreadsheet is enough. Your 90-day test is simple: do five of them run it a second and third time without being nagged?

## Rules
- Never claim features Pacer doesn't have. If asked about school accounts, rosters or grade sync, say "not yet, here's what I'm working on."
- Do not put real student names or sensitive information on the board during pilots.
- Tell every teacher the AI is labeled and can be wrong, and that they should review recaps before sharing.
